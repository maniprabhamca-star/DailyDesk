Hire your first employee

Six federal forms are involved in a first hire, and they belong to three different agencies on three different clocks: two before the first paycheck, two on a recurring schedule, one at year end. Only one of them is ever sent to the IRS by you on the day you hire.

What this page does not cover. State and local forms — including your state's own withholding certificate, new-hire reporting and unemployment-insurance registration, none of which are in our library yet. The mistake this set produces

USCIS rejects a superseded I-9 edition outright — it is the one agency in this set where filing last year's version is not merely untidy. The IRS generally still accepts an older revision; USCIS returns the form. That is why every page here prints the agency's own word for its version marker — Edition date, Rev., Form year — instead of flattening all three into "version".

Why one row says Overlay

Most USCIS PDFs are encrypted, so pdf-lib reads 0 fields and nothing can be written into them by name. That form still arrives in order, in the place-anywhere filler — it just is not pre-filled, and the row says so. The alternative is a packet that quietly skips a form or claims a fill it did not do.

04

Three rules the build enforces

A page that answers "what do I need to hire someone" is one step from practising law and tax. These are not notes — each is a test that fails the build.

Rule 1

There is no "required"

A form's relevance is always, usually or sometimes, and anything other than always must carry a condition. The words "required", "you must" and "you need to file" fail the test suite. The page states who files what; it never tells anyone what they have to do.

Rule 2

What is missing is above the fold

notCovered is a mandatory field, rendered under the hero rather than in a footer. Naming the state withholding certificate on a hiring page is also the clearest demand signal we will ever get for which Phase 1 matrix to build first.

Rule 3

Two clocks, never merged

The weekly job checks revisions, not sets. If an agency retires a form, the composed page is wrong and every number on it still verifies drifts=0. So the forms carry CHECKED_ON and the set carries its own reviewedOn, and both are printed.
