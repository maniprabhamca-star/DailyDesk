PDF to EPUB

A PDF is a fixed page. An EPUB is a river of text that reflows to whatever screen it lands on. So this tool isn’t a re-wrap — the layout has to be read back into structure, and the page’s furniture has to be thrown away, or the book reads like a photocopy.

Tidy for reading

Three artefacts make almost every converted e-book look photocopied. They all come from the same source: page furniture that only made sense when the page was fixed. Turning the option off leaves the text exactly as the PDF had it.

Running heads and folios

A line that sits at the same edge on half the pages or more is furniture. Documents under four pages are left alone — too little evidence, and a false positive costs a real heading.

Hyphens at the break

Rejoined after the pages are stitched, not before: a word is just as likely to break across a page turn as across a line, and only the joined text shows both halves.

How chapters are decided

Each rung is tried only when the one above finds nothing usable. The reader can override the whole ladder from the Chapters menu.

  1. The PDF’s own bookmarks

    The best source there is: a person chose those divisions. Text before the first bookmark is kept as front matter rather than dropped.

  2. The headings we detect

    Falls to the shallowest heading level that yields a sensible count, so a book with parts breaks at the parts and a report that only uses one level still breaks.

  3. Fixed page blocks

    A safety net for long documents. One 300-page XHTML file makes cheap e-reader hardware crawl, so anything past 40 pages is split even in single-chapter mode.

What shipped, and what didn’t

Shipped

  • EPUB 3 with an EPUB 2 NCX alongside it — older Kobo and Kindle’s converter still read the NCX
  • Cover from page one, editable title, author and language
  • Headings, bullet and numbered lists, and tables carried across
  • Paragraphs healed where a page break cut them in half
  • Pictures, pulled off each page in draw order and placed back at the same point in the text
  • Right-to-left books — Arabic, Hebrew, Urdu and the rest get the text direction, the layout and the page-turn direction they should have
  • Chapter list and live preview before you commit

Still open

  • Footnotes come through as loose text, not linked notes
  • Multi-column art direction — a magazine spread scrambles when it reflows
  • The cover is always page one; you can’t supply your own
  • Scanned PDFs have no text to reflow — the tool detects this and sends you to OCR
