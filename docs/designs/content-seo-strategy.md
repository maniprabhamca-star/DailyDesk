# Content strategy — closing the page-count gap without writing a blog

**Written:** 2026-08-29 · Follows from
[competitor-pdfnet-gap-analysis.md](competitor-pdfnet-gap-analysis.md).

---

## 1. The reframe

The pdf.net finding was that 423 of their 471 pages are content, not tools. The
natural reading is "we need to start doing content." That reading is wrong.

**We already have the machine.** 80 of our 176 pages are content, produced from
data files by exactly the same pattern they use:

| Family | Pages | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Passport & visa photo specs | 46 | `lib/passport-specs.ts` |
| Developer tools | 16 | `lib/dev-tools.ts` |
| Bank statement guides | 11 | `lib/bank-statements.ts` |
| Sector pages (`/for/…`) | 4 | `lib/sectors.ts` |
| Statement workflows | 3 | `lib/statement-workflows.ts` |
| Competitor alternatives | 4 + `/compare` | hand-written |

So the gap is not a missing capability. It is that the machine is running at
about a fifth of its useful scale, and its largest family has a defect that
would be multiplied rather than diluted by adding more pages.

---

## 2. Why their forms library works — the pattern worth copying

Four properties, and all four have to hold:

1. **One page per NAMED thing people search for.** Not "tax forms" — `Form W-9`.
   The name is the query, and the query has volume, intent, and no ambiguity.
2. **The page answers the question AND does the job.** Facts, deadlines,
   penalties, *and* the fillable file with the editor attached.
3. **It is generated from data, not written by hand.** 225 pages, one template.
4. **The intent is already transactional.** Nobody searches "W-9" idly. They
   have to fill one in, today.

**The test for any new content family:** does the page end with the reader
using a tool? If it ends with them having read something, it is a blog post,
and a blog post is a content-team commitment we cannot staff.

Our passport pages and bank pages already pass all four. That is why those are
the families to grow.

## 3. Fix before you scale — the passport problem

`lib/passport-specs.ts` says this out loud, in a comment above the editorial
block:

> Germany, France, Poland and a dozen others are all 35×45 mm with a 70–80%
> head, so every generated sentence about them is identical and Google treats
> the pages as duplicates of one another.

Two measurable problems in the biggest family:

- **21 of 46 specs are unverified.** `VERIFIED_SPECS` lists 25. The rest use
  generic ICAO 35×45 values. Five countries are still generated by
  `schengen()`, which encodes the *visa* standard and has already been wrong
  six times out of ten (see the passport spec audit in memory).
- **The 35×45 cluster deduplicates.** Search Console reported "Duplicate
  without user-selected canonical". The 2026-08-23 template rewrite fixed the
  distinct-spec countries (Canada vs Japan now 52.6% similar) and could not fix
  the identical-spec cluster (Japan vs Nepal still 80.3%).

**Adding 100 more countries to this template would produce 100 more duplicates.**

The fix is already scoped at ~14 hours in
[passport-photo-editorial-scope.md](passport-photo-editorial-scope.md): five
editorial fields per country, sourced from the issuing authority, that
differentiate two countries publishing the same dimensions.

**Priority 1: 0 new pages, unlocks the 46 that exist.**

## 4. Deepen what already converts

### A. Bank statement guides: 11 → ~40 ⭐ highest revenue leverage

Today: SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Kotak, PNB, Bank of Baroda, Canara, Union Bank,
IDFC First, Yes Bank.

Every one of these pages funnels into the **flagship paid tool**. The sitemap
comment already calls this family "the flagship's SEO engine". More pages here
is the most direct line from content to revenue we have.

Obvious additions, all with real search volume and all recurring:

- **Banks:** IndusInd, Federal, RBL, Bandhan, AU Small Finance, IDBI, Central
  Bank, Indian Bank, UCO, South Indian Bank, Karnataka Bank, CSB, DCB, Standard
  Chartered India, HSBC India, Citi India, Deutsche India
- **Payments / neobanks:** Paytm Payments Bank, Airtel Payments Bank, Fi, Jupiter,
  Slice
- **Credit card statements** — a genuinely separate query and a separate parsing
  problem: Amex, HDFC card, SBI Card, ICICI card, Axis card
- **Not banks but the same intent:** Razorpay settlements, PhonePe/GPay
  transaction history, Zerodha ledger

Each page needs: how to download the statement from *that* portal, whether it
arrives password-protected and what the password format is, what the layout
quirks are, and the converter. The first two are the parts nobody else writes
and the ones people actually search for.

### B. Sector pages: 4 → ~12

Today: legal, accountants, healthcare, schools. The argument — "your file never
leaves the device" — lands hardest on people with a professional duty of
confidentiality. Missing: HR/recruitment, banking & NBFC, insurance, government
& public sector, architecture/engineering, journalism, non-profits, tax
practitioners (CAs).

### C. Alternatives pages: 4 → ~10

Today: Adobe Acrobat, iLovePDF, Smallpdf, Sejda. These are the money subset of
what pdf.net does with a blog, without the blog. Missing and worth having:
PDF24, pdfFiller, Foxit, Soda PDF, Canva (for the PDF-editing query), and
pdf.net itself.

Keep to the policy already recorded in memory: name competitors, keep claims
precise and sourced, scope "uploads to a server" to *online* tools.

## 5. The one genuinely new family — Indian statutory forms

pdf.net owns US tax forms: 131 pages on IRS and USCIS documents. **Copying that
would be worthless to us** — wrong country, and they are three years ahead.

**Nobody owns the Indian equivalent**, and it is a bigger annual-recurrence
market than the US one:

- **Income tax:** ITR-1 (Sahaj), ITR-2, ITR-3, ITR-4 (Sugam), Form 16, Form 16A,
  Form 26AS, AIS/TIS, Form 15G, Form 15H, Form 10E, Form 12BB
- **GST:** GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-9, GST REG-01
- **Identity:** Form 49A (PAN), Form 49AA, Aadhaar enrolment/correction
- **Employment:** EPF Form 19, Form 10C, Form 31, UAN forms, Form 13 (transfer)
- **Company:** SPICe+, DIR-3 KYC, AOC-4, MGT-7
- **Banking:** KYC update, FATCA/CRS declaration, nomination forms

Why this is the right family for us specifically:

- It **connects to the flagship.** Someone converting a bank statement is
  usually doing it for a return. Statement → ITR is one journey, and we would
  own both ends of it.
- The current search results for these are dominated by ad-heavy scraper sites
  with stale PDF versions. The bar is low and the queries are enormous.
- It is the same template as the passport family: name, what it is, who files
  it, deadline, penalty, how to fill it, the file, the tool.

**Prerequisite:** get the passport template's differentiation right first, or
this family inherits the same duplicate problem on day one.

---

## 6. Deliberately not doing

- **A general blog.** 198 posts is a content team. A stale blog is worse than
  none: it dates the product and dilutes the tool pages that actually convert.
  The "alternatives" pages are the profitable 5% of what a blog would do; take
  those and leave the rest.
- **Multi-language.** Seven locales is roughly 7× the surface and 7× the
  maintenance, including translated FAQ copy that has to stay true as tools
  change. Revisit after the revenue flip, and if so, Hindi first — it matches
  where the flagship's market is, not where the biggest search index is.

## 7. Order of work

| # | Item | New pages | Why it is in this position |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Passport differentiation + verify the 21 | **0** | Unlocks 46 existing pages. Scaling a deduplicated template multiplies the defect. |
| 2 | Banks 11 → 40 | ~29 | Straightest line from a page to the paid tool. |
| 3 | Alternatives 4 → 10 | ~6 | Cheap, high intent, template already exists. |
| 4 | Sectors 4 → 12 | ~8 | Cheap, and the privacy argument is our actual differentiator. |
| 5 | Indian statutory forms | ~40–60 | Biggest prize, but only after #1 proves the template differentiates. |

Ends at roughly **265 pages** — still fewer than pdf.net's 471, on a catalogue
three times the size, with every page ending at a tool rather than at an ad.
