A bar at the bottom, where the thumb already is

Three ways to give DiemDesk a bottom bar on phones, the one I would build, and the two things about mobile browsers that decide whether it feels native or broken.

The ideaWhy this is worth doing

On a phone today, every route to another tool goes through the hamburger at the top right. That is the corner furthest from a right thumb and completely out of reach for a left one. Meanwhile the thing people came to do — put a file in — is somewhere down the page.

A bottom bar fixes both at once, and it is the single change that makes a website feel like an app. It is also already the plan: the app-shell note names “bottom-or-drawer nav on mobile” as the mobile half of the persistent shell.

The recommendationBuild B now, C next

B, because the centre slot should be a verb rather than a destination

Four of the five slots are places you go. The fifth is the thing you came to do. Giving open a file the raised centre position means the primary action is reachable from every page in the app with one thumb, which is exactly what a native app would do and exactly what a website usually fails to do.

It also solves a real problem that has nothing to do with looks: today, on a tool page, there is no way to start a different job without scrolling back up and opening the hamburger.

The hard partTwo things that decide whether it feels native

1. The home indicator and the browser’s own bar. iOS Safari and Chrome both put chrome at the bottom of the screen, and iPhones reserve a strip for the home indicator. A bar that ignores this sits under the browser UI and is untappable. It needs padding-bottom: env(safe-area-inset-bottom) and a matching viewport-fit=cover, and the page needs bottom padding of its own so the last row of content is not hidden behind the bar.
2. It must not appear on desktop, and must not double up. Below sm only. The desktop header already carries all five destinations, and two navigations visible at once is how an app-shell starts feeling like a website wearing a costume.

What I would NOT do

  • Hide it on scroll. It saves 56 pixels and costs the one thing a bottom bar is for: being there without being looked for.
  • Put five destinations in and call the fifth “More”. A More tab is an admission the other four were chosen badly.
  • Animate the centre button on load. It draws the eye once and irritates on every visit after.

ThenFavourites, and why they come second

The one-click favourites idea is right, and it is option C. It needs two things B does not: a way to pin a tool, and somewhere to keep the list. Recents already exist and cost nothing, so the Recent slot ships with real content on day one while pinning is designed properly rather than bolted on.

The natural gesture: long-press a tool card to pin it, and the Recent sheet grows a pinned row above the automatic one. That is a small, self-contained follow-up — not a reason to delay the bar.

Specimens are recreations at phone proportions, not screenshots. Slot names and destinations match the routes that exist today.
