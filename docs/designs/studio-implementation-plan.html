Studio, and what belongs in it

The implementation plan for the design studio concept of 13 July 2026 — plus the question that reopened it: should we carry a template catalogue like pdfFiller’s, and if so, where does it live?

A private, no-watermark studio inside your document workspace — make the thing, on your device, and send it straight into the tools you already use here.

Canva makes you upload. Studio keeps your work on your device. That sentence is the entire product, and everything below either serves it or gets cut.

01

The line we do not cross

The failure mode here is obvious and it is worth naming before any scope is written down.

We will not out-spend Canva on template volume, asset libraries or collaboration. They have thousands of designers and a decade of head start. Any plan whose success depends on catching up on count has already failed.

What we have that they structurally cannot copy without dismantling their own product:

  • On device. The design never leaves the browser. For a certificate with someone’s name on it, an invoice with your client list, or a letterhead with your address, that is not a preference.
  • No watermark, ever. Not a trial limitation we later remove — a thing we never do.
  • Deterministic. The same input gives the same output. No server-side “improvements” between sessions.
  • Offline. It is a PWA. It works on a plane.
  • Integrated. Export straight into compress, PDF, QR, background remover, and a saved brand kit — tools that already exist here rather than a second app to learn.
The one-line position

Not “a cheaper Canva”. The design tool for work you would rather not upload.

02

Two template families, and they are not the same product

The pdfFiller catalogue mixes them freely. We should not, because they need different editors, carry different risk, and sell to different people.

Family A

Design templates

~40 at launch

Visual work on a canvas. Laid out by eye, exported as an image or a PDF. This is what the July concept described, and it needs the canvas editor.

Family B

Document templates

~24 at launch

Text-shaped work with fields and a layout that should not move. These are what your screenshot showed. They do not need a canvas editor at all — they need a form-shaped fill and a clean PDF out, which is closer to tools we already have.

And a third group that should not ship

Legal instruments — bills of sale, agreements, by-laws, affidavits, powers of attorney, leases. pdfFiller and US Legal Forms both carry them, and US Legal Forms is explicitly a legal-forms business with the positioning and the counsel to match. We are not, and our own standing invariant across the whole site is that we supply a tool, not advice. Handing someone a blank bill of sale is a materially different act from handing them a blank IRS form that the IRS itself publishes. Recommend: not in Phase 1, and not at all without counsel signing off on the category.

Why this belongs in Studio and not in the forms library

This was decision D5 on the forms plan, and it is worth restating because the two look superficially alike. A government form has an issuing authority, a revision the authority publishes, and a file we can re-read every Monday to prove we are still right about it. A cover-letter template has none of those things — there is no authority to cite, no revision to drift, nothing to verify.

Every piece of machinery that makes /us-forms credible — the live fetch, the revision marker, the field count, the date checked, the weekly job — applies to exactly none of these templates. Shelving them side by side would teach readers that our forms are “templates too”, which is the one impression the forms library exists to avoid.

03

The build

Three phases. Phase 1 ships without a single AI call, deliberately — the concept doc already set that rule and it still holds.

PHASE 1

The canvas, and enough templates to be useful

~64 templatesno AIMVP
  • Canvas editor — text, shapes, images, layers, align and distribute, colour, fonts, snapping and guides, undo. Built on the owner’s existing Bonafide banner studio rather than from nothing.
  • Family A: ~40 design templates across the seven categories above, with preset canvas sizes per platform.
  • Family B: ~24 document templates — and these route through the existing fill-and-export path, not the canvas. Building them twice would be the first scope mistake.
  • Export PNG, JPG, PDF — reusing our compress and PDF-export engines rather than new ones.
PHASE 2

Power, and the integrations only we can offer

Pro levers
  • Brand kit — logo, colours, fonts saved once, applied in one click. Already on the Pro differentiator list independently of Studio.
  • Magic resize — one design into every platform size. On device, so there is no per-render cost to us and no cap to impose.
  • Background remover integration, and batch across a set of designs — the on-device batch that competitors cannot match without paying for every render.
PHASE 3

AI, behind the budget kill-switch

cost-gatednot before 1 & 2

Text-to-image, AI background, copy suggestions — all of it behind the existing AI guardrails: cheap model, per-user cap, and a global budget pegged to revenue with a kill-switch. Studio is the feature most likely to blow an AI budget, because image generation is the expensive kind, so it is last and it is metered from the first line of code rather than retrofitted.

Keep these numbers apart from the forms numbers

The forms plan targets ~190 verified government forms. Studio targets ~135 templates. They are different things measured in different ways — a form is verified against an agency, a template is made by us — and adding them into one headline figure would be the first step toward treating them as one library, which §2 argues against.

05

What could go wrong

Three, in the order they are likely to bite.

  • Scope creep — the big one. Canva is enormous and every missing feature will feel like a gap. The phased output list above is the defence; anything not on it waits for a second opinion rather than a sprint.
  • Licensing. Every template, font, icon and image original or licence-clean for commercial use. This has bitten projects far larger than ours, and a template catalogue is exactly where it bites.
  • Legal-template liability. Covered in §2. The temptation will return the moment someone sees the search volume on “free bill of sale template”.
