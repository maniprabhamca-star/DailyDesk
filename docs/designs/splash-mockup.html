Two splashes, two right places

A full-screen splash on every web visit would slow a fast site. These are the two spots where a splash is correct and reads premium: the installed app launch, and a one-time welcome for brand-new web visitors. Hit Replay to watch each.

What launching the installed Android/desktop app looks like — exactly Gmail’s “Google Workspace” screen. Driven entirely by the web manifest; web visitors never see it.
How it’s built
name DiemDesk
background_color the indigo ground above
theme_color matches the app bar
icons the 512px lifted-D mark, centred by the OS

The OS renders this automatically from manifest.webmanifest the moment the app launches — no code, no delay we control, no effect on the website. We already ship a manifest; this is a few fields plus one icon.

My recommendation

Ship the installed-app splash regardless — it’s nearly free, it’s what big brands do, and it lands exactly when your TWA/Android app ships. Pure upside.

For the web, go with Option A (minimal), first-visit-only. It gives the brand a confident hello without ever taxing a returning visitor or a search crawler. Option B is lovely but the extra half-second is a cost every new visitor pays — better saved for a campaign or the app onboarding.

The guardrails I’d build in

Once only (localStorage flag) · skippable (tap/scroll dismisses instantly) · never blocks content (the page is already rendered underneath) · respects reduced-motion (no animation for those who ask) · never on a shared tool link (someone landing on /compress-pdf from Google sees the tool, not a splash).
