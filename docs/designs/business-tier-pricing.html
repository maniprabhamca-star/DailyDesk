Two products,
one plan

We have a $5.98 consumer subscription and a $19 single-tool subscription, and an accountant who needs both has to buy both. The business tier is not a new price to invent. It is the merge of the two we already published.

Verdict one — the price

Business is $19 / month, and that is already our published number.

Statements Pro is $19 today for 300 statement pages and nothing else. Rename it Business, fold all of Pro into it, and no published price goes up while every buyer gets more. The two-subscription problem disappears without a price to defend.

Verdict two — the shape

Three tiers. No seats at launch.

Seats need an organisation model, invites, member management and Stripe quantity billing — weeks of work for a tier with no customers yet. Ship Business as single-user, add a Firm tier when there is someone asking for the second seat.

The worst case matters more than the typical one, because the worst case is what a cost control has to survive. A Business user who maxes 200 AI actions, escalates every statement page and fills the vault lands near $9.55 of cost against $19 of revenue — 50% margin. That is inside the rule we set ourselves (blended variable cost under 40–50% of price), but only because of the escalation cap in §06. Without that cap it falls through the floor.

05

India pricing

₹1,499, which is also already published. The statement converter is India-first and its buyer is a CA.

₹1,499 is not a conversion of $19 — at today's rate that would be around ₹1,690, and the site's own comment in lib/currency.ts already says why pricing is a product decision rather than an FX calculation. ₹1,499 is the figure in the statement-converter spec, it is on the live pricing page, and it stays.

The annual is ₹14,999 (₹1,250/mo effective), mirroring Pro's structure of roughly two months free. One consequence worth stating plainly: an Indian Business subscriber nets us about ₹1,100 a month after costs, against ₹310 for an Indian Pro subscriber — the same 3.5× ratio as the dollar tiers, so the tier works in both currencies without a separate argument.

Trap one — the silent downgrade

Every plan === 'pro' check in the codebase becomes false for a Business subscriber the moment a third tier exists. Someone paying $19 would be denied batch, size limits and Edit PDF — every Pro feature — and the build would be green, the tests would pass, and we would hear about it from the customer. B1 is first for that reason, and the guard test is not optional.

Trap two — the escalation ladder has no ceiling

The statement engine's design escalates a page to Haiku, then to Sonnet, whenever the balance check fails. That is the right design — the balance check is a free accuracy oracle — but it has no per-account ceiling. A corrupt 300-page scan where every balance fails bills us Sonnet 300 times. The per-account monthly tier-3 cap in B3 is what makes the margin table above true rather than aspirational.

07

Open decisions

Five. P1 and P2 shape the launch; the rest can wait for a customer.

P1Is Business $19, or $24 with seats?

$19 reuses a published price and ships without an organisation model. $24 is under the $24.98 sum of parts, adds five members, and needs invites, member management and Stripe quantity billing first.

Recommend $19, single user. A price can rise when seats arrive. A tier that waits three weeks for a feature nobody has asked for yet earns nothing in the meantime.

P2Does the credit pack survive?

$4.99 for 20 pages is $0.25 a page — four times the Business per-page rate. It exists for the person who converts statements once a year at tax time and will never subscribe.

Recommend keeping it. It is the only thing we sell to someone who refuses a subscription, and it anchors $19 as the sensible choice. But it must stop being a peer of Business on the pricing page — it is a footnote under the table, not a third card.

P3Does the founding offer apply to Business?

The live page offers the first 1,000 members $4.99/mo for life. Extending that to Business means a lifetime $15.83 subscriber.

Recommend no — founding is a Pro offer only, and the page must say so before anyone can buy. A lifetime discount on the tier that carries the margin is the one pricing mistake that cannot be undone.

P4Does the redaction certificate move from Pro to Business?

It is a professional-liability feature — a signed record that a redaction actually removed the bytes. The person who needs it is defending a disclosure, which is a Business buyer. It is currently a listed Pro pillar.

Owner's call. It is unbuilt, so moving it costs nothing today and costs a broken promise later. If it moves, it moves before anyone can pay.

P5When does a Firm tier appear?

DocuClipper includes every seat. A five-person practice comparing us will notice. The answer is a Firm tier — more pages, members, one invoice — around $49.

Recommend: when the first person asks for a second seat. Not before. Three tiers is already one more than we can currently sell.

08

What we are deliberately not doing

Recorded so these do not get re-litigated in six months.

  • Metering by page, the way DocuClipper does Their whole pricing page is a page counter. It is honest and it scales with their server cost — but our statement parsing runs on the customer's own device, so a page costs us nothing unless the balance check fails. Charging per page would be charging for a cost we do not have, and it would make the customer count their work before doing it.
  • Putting a daily limit on free in-browser tools Smallpdf charges $15 a month partly for the removal of a limit they invented. We have said in public that every in-browser tool is unlimited at every price. That line is worth more than the conversions a cap would force.
  • A per-seat price at launch Seats are the correct eventual answer and the wrong first move. See P1 and P5.
  • Raising Pro to make room for Business Pro at $5.98 undercuts every comparable consumer tool and matches iLovePDF exactly. It is doing its job as the bottom of the funnel. The business tier is built above it, not by moving it.
  • A fourth paid product We already made this mistake once by letting the statement converter become its own subscription instead of a tier. The rule going forward: a new capability joins a tier, or it is a credit pack. It does not become a fourth thing to subscribe to.
09

What happens first

None of this earns anything while WAITLIST_MODE is true. That flag, and the Stripe prices behind it, are the only things between this document and revenue — and both are owner actions, not engineering.

So the honest sequence is: build B1 through B8 now, because the third tier must exist before the switch is flipped rather than after — a pricing page that changes shape a week after the first customer pays is a refund conversation. Then flip the flag once, with three tiers on the page and the plan checks swept.

The forms work runs in parallel and does not touch any of this. It is the funnel; this is the till.
